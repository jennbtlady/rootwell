# Maple Ledger personas for usability testing

Three research-based personas for testing with tiny nonprofits: 1 to 5 staff, budgets under about $1M. Each persona has role realities, goals, fears, devices, accessibility notes, test scenarios, and a recruiting screener.

How to read this page:

- **Sourced** facts have a link right under them.
- **Assumption** marks a detail we believe but have not proven. Check it in sessions.
- Quotes are **illustrative**. They are written to sound like real people. They are not real quotes.
- Reddit could not be reached by our research tools while this was written. The "real complaints" below come from vendor help forums and software review sites instead. Before recruiting, spend an hour reading r/nonprofit and r/fundraising by hand. Confirm or replace the complaint themes and quotes.

## What the research says about tiny shops

These facts shape all three personas.

1. **Most giving comes from people, not foundations.** Individuals gave about 66% of the $592.5 billion given in the US in 2024.
   Source: https://givingusa.org/giving-usa-2025-u-s-charitable-giving-grew-to-592-50-billion-in-2024-lifted-by-stock-market-gain

2. **Donors are getting harder to keep.** Overall donor retention was about 43% in 2025. Donor counts fell about 3.6% while dollars rose about 5%. The drop in donor counts has run since 2021. (This is a vendor summary of the Fundraising Effectiveness Project report.)
   Source: https://www.donorperfect.com/nonprofit-technology-blog/fundraising-software/afp-fep-q4-2025/
   New donor retention was 14% and micro donor ($1 to $100) retention was 21.3% through Q3 2025.
   Source: https://bloomerang.com/blog/donor-retention

3. **Money and staff are stretched.** 36% of nonprofits ended 2024 with a deficit, the highest in ten years of the survey. More than two-thirds struggle to hire enough staff with the right skills. Only 12% of groups under $250,000 offer health insurance.
   Source: https://nff.org/wp-content/uploads/NFF-2025-Survey-Executive-Summary-1.pdf

4. **Small groups have no tech person.** 95% of nonprofits under $1M have no senior technology leader, on staff or on contract.
   Source: https://word.nten.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/2024-DMS-Study.pdf (NTEN and Karen Graham Consulting, 2024)

5. **Training, not software, is the usual gap.** NTEN says staff often have the tools but not the training. 15% of groups said their donor database actually hindered fundraising. Basic reports, ease of use, and import and export were among the seven features rated most important.
   Source: https://word.nten.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/2024-DMS-Study.pdf

6. **Low staff time blocks switching.** The top barriers to buying a new database were low staff time to evaluate it, low staff time to set it up, and fear of disruption.
   Source: https://word.nten.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/2024-DMS-Study.pdf

7. **QuickBooks does not make donor statements.** People on Intuit's own help forum say QuickBooks Online cannot produce year-end donor statements. Accountants there suggest a separate donor database.
   Source: https://quickbooks.intuit.com/learn-support/en-us/account-management/nonprofit-here-we-need-to-send-out-year-end-donor-statements/00/213660

8. **The IRS letter rules are specific.** A donor needs a written acknowledgment for any single gift of $250 or more. It must say whether the donor got goods or services in return.
   Source: https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/charitable-contributions-written-acknowledgments

9. **Phones matter, especially for people with less money.** 16% of US adults have a smartphone but no home broadband. That rises to 29% in households under $30,000. 79% of adults 65 and older own a smartphone.
   Source: https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/mobile/ (2026 survey)

## Persona 1: Dana, the executive director who does everything

**One line:** Runs the organization, raises the money, and enters the gifts at night because nobody else will.

### Role realities

- Leads a group with 2 to 4 staff and a budget near $400,000. **Assumption** for the exact size.
- Does fundraising, gift entry, grant reports, payroll sign-off, and board prep. There is no development director right now, or there is one who just left.
- When a small group's development job is empty, it stays empty a long time. The median vacancy was 12 months at groups under $1M, against 6 months overall.
  Source: https://www.giarts.org/sites/default/files/Underdeveloped-National-Study-of-Challenges-Facing-Nonprofit-Fundraising.pdf (UnderDeveloped, CompassPoint, 2013, page 5)
- 32% of groups under $1M had no fundraising database at all, and 31% had no fundraising plan.
  Source: same report, page 12
- 26% of executive directors called themselves novices at fundraising. 18% of those who must ask for gifts say they dislike it.
  Source: same report, page 14
- Two-thirds of executive directors in 2011 expected to leave their job within five years. They named unstable finances, weak boards, and work-life balance.
  Source: https://www.giarts.org/node/2448 (Daring to Lead 2011) and https://nonprofitpro.com/aggregatedcontent/two-thirds-nonprofit-executive-directors-plan-leave-within-5-years-survey-reveals
- The average executive director in the UnderDeveloped sample was 53. In BoardSource's 2021 report, 49% of chief executives were 55 or older and 74% were women.
  Sources: UnderDeveloped page 32; https://boardsource.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/2021-Leading-with-Intent-DEI-Report.pdf (page 3)
- Note: the CompassPoint studies are from 2011 and 2013, but remain the best national data on these roles.

### Goals

- Get every gift in, thanked, and matched to the bank deposit without staying up late.
- Hand the board a clean one-page picture before each meeting.
- Leave a system the next person can run without her. **Assumption** that this is a stated goal; test it.

### Frustrations and fears

- "The database person left." The last development director kept the rules in her head. Nobody knows what "Fund 3" means. **Assumption**, drawn from the brief's research and the vacancy data above.
- Year-end statements. QuickBooks cannot make them, so she builds them by hand in January (see fact 7 above).
- Being wrong in front of the board. A number on the board report does not match the bank. **Assumption.**
- Breaking something she cannot fix, with no tech person to call (see fact 4 above).

### Tech comfort and devices

- Comfortable with email, Google Sheets or Excel, and QuickBooks. Not comfortable with imports, formulas she did not write, or settings pages. **Assumption.**
- Works on a laptop at the office for gift entry. Checks things on her phone between meetings and at events. **Assumption.**
- Gift entry often happens in a burst: a stack of checks, once or twice a week. **Assumption.**

### Accessibility to test

- Age spread is wide; about half of executive directors are 55 or older (source above). Test with browser zoom at 200% and with larger system text. Do not assume poor vision; do check that nothing breaks when text gets bigger.
- Keyboard flow for gift entry. Someone entering 20 checks will tab, not click. Watch for focus that jumps or gets lost.
- Interrupted sessions. She will leave a batch half done. Check that work is not lost.

### What success looks like

- She enters a stack of checks in one sitting, the total matches the deposit slip, and she trusts it.
- She sends year-end statements in one afternoon, not one week.
- She could hand the login to a new hire with a one-page note.

### Illustrative quotes

> "I don't need fancy. I need the total to match the deposit slip so I can go home." (illustrative)

> "Our last development person kept everything in her head. When she left, so did the rules." (illustrative)

> "January is statement month. I dread it." (illustrative)

### Test scenarios for Dana

Prepare the sample organization. Give the participant a printed deposit slip and copies of 6 checks with invented donors. Make one check total wrong on purpose, so the slip and checks disagree by $25.

**D1. Enter a stack of checks** (guided gift entry into a dated batch)

- Read aloud: "Here are six checks from this week's mail and the deposit slip you took to the bank. Please record these gifts the way you would on a normal Tuesday."
- Success: all six gifts are in one batch dated for this deposit, with fund, campaign, and check number where the organization requires them.
- Watch for: does she find where a batch starts? Does she read the standards messages ("Enter the check number") or fight them? Does she use Tab through the form? How long per check? Does she try to enter a gift outside a batch?

**D2. Make the numbers match the bank** (posting a batch against a deposit total)

- Read aloud: "Your bookkeeper needs to know these gifts are final and match what went into the bank. Please finish this off so nobody can change it by accident."
- Success: she enters the deposit total, sees the $25 mismatch, finds the wrong gift, fixes it, and posts the batch.
- Watch for: does she understand the mismatch message? Does she know which gift is wrong? Does she understand that posting locks the gifts? Does that feel safe or scary?

**D3. Add a new donor you met at an event** (add a constituent with the live duplicate warning)

- Read aloud: "At last night's event you met someone who wants to give. Here is the card she gave you. Please add her." Use a card for a person who already exists in the sample data under a slightly different name or email.
- Success: she notices the warning that this person may already exist, opens the existing record, and does not create a second one.
- Watch for: does she see the warning at all? Does she trust it? Does she know what to do next? Test this one on her phone at 375px width.

**D4. Get year-end letters out** (year-end giving statements)

- Read aloud: "It's January. Donors need a record of what they gave last year for their taxes. Please get that to everyone who gave."
- Success: she makes statements for the right tax year, previews one, and sends email to donors with an address while printing the rest as one PDF.
- Watch for: does she know which year to pick? Does she check the "no goods or services" wording? Does she worry about sending to the wrong person? Does she look for a way to send a test first?

**D5. Share the numbers with the board** (creating the read-only board share link)

- Read aloud: "The board meets Thursday. Your treasurer wants to look at how fundraising is going before then, but she doesn't have a login. Please get her what she needs."
- Success: she finds the board sharing setting, turns on the link, decides whether donor names show, and copies the link.
- Watch for: does she understand who can see the link? Does the "hide names" choice make sense? Does she know how to turn it off later?

### Screener for Dana

1. What is your job title? (Accept: executive director, CEO, founder, or similar head of the organization.)
2. How many paid staff does your organization have, counting part-time? (Accept: 1 to 5.)
3. What was your organization's budget last year? (Accept: under about $1M.)
4. In a typical month, do you personally enter gifts or donor information? (Accept: yes.)
5. Where do you keep donor and gift records today? (Want a mix: spreadsheets, a donor database, QuickBooks only.)
6. In the last two years, has someone who managed your donor records left? (Want at least two "yes" answers in the group.)

## Persona 2: Marcus, the part-time development generalist

**One line:** The only fundraising staff person, often part-time, who is also the data person, the thank-you writer, and the newsletter editor.

### Role realities

- Works 20 to 30 hours a week. Title might be development director, development and communications manager, or development coordinator. **Assumption** for hours and titles.
- Owns the donor records, imports, thank-you letters, appeals, the event, and sometimes social media. **Assumption.**
- Development directors at groups under $1M were the most likely to plan to leave: 57% expected to leave within two years, and 27% expected to leave fundraising as a career.
  Source: https://www.giarts.org/sites/default/files/Underdeveloped-National-Study-of-Challenges-Facing-Nonprofit-Fundraising.pdf (pages 6 and 7)
- Average development director pay at groups under $1M was about $49,000 in that study, against $80,000 at $5M to $10M groups.
  Source: same report, page 10
- Only 41% of development directors called their partnership with the executive director strong.
  Source: same report, page 15
- 82% of executive directors at groups under $1M said board engagement in fundraising was not enough.
  Source: same report, page 13
- Average age of development directors in that study was 47. 79% were women.
  Source: same report, page 32

### Goals

- Clean records he can trust, so appeals reach the right people once.
- Thank every donor fast. Bloomerang's own data links a thank-you call to higher second-gift rates (37% without a call, 55% with one). This is a vendor study and shows a link, not proof of cause.
  Source: https://info.bloomerang.com/rs/618-WGI-459/images/10.08%20-%20Increase%20Retention%20for%20Giving%20Tuesday%20Donors.pdf?version=0
- Pull a lapsed donor list without asking anyone for help.
- Leave the records better than he found them. **Assumption.**

### Frustrations and fears

- Inherited mess. Two or three spreadsheets, the same donor three ways, no notes on what the columns mean. **Assumption**, consistent with the brief's research.
- Imports. Reviewers of small-shop databases say the first import was hard and sync with QuickBooks still needed journal entries.
  Source: https://www.capterra.com/p/97513/DonorSnap/reviews/?page=5
- Merging two people who are not the same person, and not being able to undo it. **Assumption.**
- Thank-you letters that go out late or twice. **Assumption.**

### Tech comfort and devices

- Most comfortable of the three. Uses spreadsheets daily, knows filters and sorting, may know VLOOKUP. **Assumption.**
- Works on a laptop, sometimes from home on a slower connection. Uses a phone at events. **Assumption.**
- Does big data jobs (imports, cleanups) in quiet blocks, often on a weekend. **Assumption**, matches the brief's "Saturday project" idea.

### Accessibility to test

- Long tables: duplicates queue, import mapping, reports. Check that column headers stay readable and that a screen reader can tell which row is which.
- Keyboard-only use of the merge page. Can he choose a field from each side without a mouse?
- Low bandwidth: upload of a 5,000-row file on a home connection. Does progress show? What happens if the tab closes?

### What success looks like

- He imports the old spreadsheet in one sitting and can undo it if it looks wrong.
- The Data Health number goes up after a cleanup, and he can show that to his boss.
- The thank-you queue is empty by Friday.

### Illustrative quotes

> "I found three Margarets. Same address. I have no idea which one is right." (illustrative)

> "Every import I've ever done, I held my breath on the last click." (illustrative)

> "If I can't pull a lapsed list myself, I'm just going to keep my own spreadsheet. Again." (illustrative)

### Test scenarios for Marcus

Prepare the messy import file from Phase 3 (invented people only). Plant at least two duplicate pairs and one pair that looks alike but is two real people.

**M1. Bring in the old spreadsheet** (five-step import wizard with rollback)

- Read aloud: "Your organization kept donors in this spreadsheet before. Please bring it into the new system so you can stop using the spreadsheet."
- Success: he uploads, matches columns, reviews possible matches to existing donors, fixes or accepts problem rows, and finishes.
- Watch for: does he understand each step's purpose? Does he read warnings about bad dates, refunds, and zero amounts? Does he notice the file matched existing donors?
- Follow-up task: "Your boss looks at it and says the campaign names are all wrong. Please put things back the way they were before you started." Success: he finds and runs the rollback, and believes it worked.

**M2. Clean up repeat records** (duplicates queue, merge, 30-day undo)

- Read aloud: "Some donors seem to be in here more than once. Please clean up the ones you're sure about."
- Success: he merges a true duplicate, picks the right address, and marks the look-alike pair as two different people.
- Watch for: does he check gifts before merging? Does he understand which record survives? Does he know he can undo?
- Follow-up task: "You realize one of those was a mistake. Please fix it." Success: he finds merge history and undoes it.

**M3. Check the overall health of the records** (Data Health panel)

- Read aloud: "Your executive director asks how clean your donor records are, and what you should fix first. What would you tell her?"
- Success: he finds Data Health, explains the main problems in his own words, and follows one link to the list that fixes it.
- Watch for: does the score mean anything to him? Does he trust it? Do the counts link where he expects?

**M4. Find people who stopped giving** (saved reports with spreadsheet download: LYBUNT, SYBUNT)

- Read aloud: "You want to send a letter to people who gave last year but haven't given yet this year. Please get that list into a spreadsheet you can use for a mail merge."
- Success: he finds the right report, filters if needed, and downloads a spreadsheet.
- Watch for: does he know the terms LYBUNT and SYBUNT? If not, does the page explain them in plain words? Does he open the file and check it?

**M5. Thank this week's donors** (thank-you queue with bulk email and letter PDFs)

- Read aloud: "Gifts from last week need a thank-you. Some donors have email, some don't. Please get everyone thanked today."
- Success: he emails those with addresses, makes one PDF of letters for the rest, and marks them as thanked.
- Watch for: does he preview first? Does he notice the IRS wording changes when a donor got something in return? Does he worry about double-sending? Does he look for a way to log a phone call instead?

### Screener for Marcus

1. What is your job title? (Accept: any staff role whose main duty is fundraising or donor records.)
2. How many hours a week do you work for this organization? (Want a mix: under 30 and full-time.)
3. How many paid staff does your organization have? (Accept: 1 to 5.)
4. Have you moved donor records from one place to another in the last three years, such as from a spreadsheet into a database? (Want at least half "yes.")
5. Which of these have you done in the last six months: sent thank-you letters, pulled a list of lapsed donors, merged duplicate records? (Accept: at least two.)
6. Roughly how many donor records does your organization keep? (Accept: 200 to 10,000.)

## Persona 3: Ruth, the volunteer board treasurer

**One line:** A volunteer who reads the fundraising numbers before board meetings, rarely logs in to anything, and wants to know if the trend is good or bad.

### Role realities

- Unpaid. Spends a few hours a month on the board. Has a full-time job or is retired. **Assumption** for hours.
- Reads reports on email or a phone, often the night before the meeting. **Assumption.**
- 43% of board members are 55 or older (26% are 55 to 64, 17% are 65 or older). 51% of board chairs are 55 or older.
  Source: https://boardsource.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/2021-Leading-with-Intent-DEI-Report.pdf (page 3)
- 5% of board members reported a disability.
  Source: same, page 3
- At groups under $1M, 18% of executive directors said the board was sufficiently engaged in asking donors for money. None said "very sufficiently."
  Source: https://www.giarts.org/sites/default/files/Underdeveloped-National-Study-of-Challenges-Facing-Nonprofit-Fundraising.pdf (page 13)

### Goals

- Know in two minutes whether fundraising is on track compared with last year.
- Answer a fellow board member's question without calling the executive director.
- Trust that the numbers match the treasurer's report from QuickBooks. **Assumption.**

### Frustrations and fears

- Jargon. "Retention" and "LYBUNT" mean nothing without a plain explanation. **Assumption.**
- Different numbers from different places. Retention can be 20% by one definition and 33% by another. FEP counts a donor as retained only if they give again the next year; Bloomerang counts any later gift.
  Source: https://bloomerang.co/blog/actually-calling-donors-to-thank-them-does-make-them-more-likely-to-give-again-and-give-more/
- Seeing private donor details she should not see, or forwarding them by accident. **Assumption.**
- One more password. **Assumption.**

### Tech comfort and devices

- Ranges widely. Some treasurers are accountants who live in spreadsheets; others mostly use email and a phone. Recruit both. **Assumption.**
- Likely to open the link on a phone or tablet. Some on a smartphone with no home broadband (see fact 9 above).

### Accessibility to test

- Large system text and pinch zoom on a phone. Does the dashboard reflow at 375px and stay readable at 200% text?
- Tooltips on touch screens. Hover does not exist on a phone. Can she open and close the retention explanation with a tap? With a keyboard? With a screen reader?
- Color. Do trend arrows or colors also have words, so color is not the only signal?
- Slow connection. Does the page load fast on a weak mobile signal?

### What success looks like

- She opens the link from an email, with no login, and says in her own words whether giving is up or down.
- She can explain what "retention" means after reading the tooltip.
- She feels the page is safe to forward to another board member.

### Illustrative quotes

> "Just tell me: are we ahead of last year or behind?" (illustrative)

> "If I have to make an account, I'll ask Dana to email me a PDF instead." (illustrative)

> "Why does this say 41% when the newsletter from that other group said 20%?" (illustrative)

### Test scenarios for Ruth

Run these on the participant's own phone if possible. Send the board share link by email before the session.

**R1. Open what the executive director sent** (read-only board share link, no login)

- Read aloud: "Your executive director emailed you something to look at before Thursday's board meeting. Please open it and tell me what you're looking at."
- Success: she opens the link, sees the dashboard without signing in, and names the organization and the time period.
- Watch for: does she expect a login? Does she trust the page? How long does it take to load on her connection?

**R2. Is fundraising on track?** (board dashboard)

- Read aloud: "A fellow board member asks you: are we doing better or worse than this time last year? What would you tell them?"
- Success: she compares this year to date with last year to date for dollars and donors, and gives a clear answer.
- Watch for: does she find the comparison? Does she mix up fiscal year and calendar year? Does she look for words, not only a chart?

**R3. What does this number mean?** (FEP retention definitions in tooltips)

- Read aloud: "Find the number that tells you how many donors came back. In your own words, what does it mean?"
- Success: she opens the explanation, and says something close to "of the people who gave the year before last, this share gave again last year."
- Watch for: can she open the explanation on a phone? Does she understand why it covers the last two completed years, not this year? Does she know first-time and repeat retention are different?

**R4. Is it safe to forward?** (share link privacy)

- Read aloud: "Another board member who missed the email asks you for it. Would you forward it? Why or why not?"
- Success: she can tell whether donor names show, and makes a choice she is comfortable with.
- Watch for: does she understand that anyone with the link can see the page? Does she look for a sign of who can see it?

**R5. Does it match the treasurer's spreadsheet?** (gifts by fund download, made by staff)

- Prepare: download the gifts by fund report as staff before the session and email it to the participant.
- Read aloud: "Dana also sent this spreadsheet of gifts by fund. Do the totals agree with what you saw on the page?"
- Success: she finds the totals in both and says whether they agree, or explains why they differ.
- Watch for: do date ranges line up? Does she understand that pledges are not counted? If they differ, does she blame the software or the data?

### Screener for Ruth

1. Do you serve on a nonprofit board now? (Accept: yes, volunteer.)
2. Does that organization have 5 or fewer paid staff? (Accept: yes.)
3. Do you read fundraising or financial reports for the board? (Accept: yes, as treasurer, finance chair, or fundraising chair.)
4. How often do you log in to your organization's donor database? (Want most to say "never" or "rarely.")
5. Which device do you most often use to read email from the organization? (Want at least half to say phone or tablet.)
6. What is your age range? (Want a spread; at least two participants 55 or older. Ask this last and make it optional.)

## How many sessions to run

- Run **5 sessions per persona, 15 in all**. Five people usually find most of the big problems in a task flow. Go to 6 per persona if you can, to cover a mix of devices and comfort levels.
  Source: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/why-you-only-need-to-test-with-5-users/
- Split into **two rounds**: 3 per persona, fix the worst problems, then 2 or 3 more per persona to check the fixes.
- Length: **60 minutes** for Dana and Marcus. **30 minutes** for Ruth, who is a volunteer.
- Run Dana and Marcus sessions remote on their own laptop, with one task on their phone. Run Ruth sessions on her own phone.
- Recruit at least one participant per persona who uses a screen reader, screen magnifier, or keyboard only, if you can. Pay them the same rate.
- Pay every participant. Volunteers give time too. **Assumption** on rates: about $75 for an hour and $50 for 30 minutes, or a donation to their organization if they prefer.

## Facilitator notes

- Use only the sample organization and invented people. Never ask a participant to upload their real donor data.
- Do not say the names on the screen. Say "record," "list," or "letter," not "batch," "constituent," or "acknowledgment," unless the participant says them first.
- If someone is stuck for more than three minutes, note it as a failure, then help them move on.
- After each task, ask: "On a scale of 1 to 5, how confident are you that this is done right?" Confidence matters as much as success for people with no tech help.
